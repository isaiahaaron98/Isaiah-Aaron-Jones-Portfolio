# User research 

## Target audience

My approach to identifying representative individuals to interview is to first examine who rests within the ecosystem of the narrative I am trying to tell. Since I am analyzing the pipeline between prominent non-profit theatre organizations and the Broadway industry (with a particular focus on the production process and the ticket revenue-total expenses gap), it is important to identify individuals that possess working knowledge of the industry at large and at various levels. For the purposes of this project, this includes producers, arts administrators, and writers. By activating this approach, I will be able to gain a better understanding of whether or not my narrative is cohesive, if the data I am using is sufficient to fully explain that narrative, and how I may integrate additional contextual information/data in order to paint a fuller picture. Additionally, I will seek an “outsider’s” perspective, or someone who is not particularly familiar with the inner workings of the Broadway industry or theatre as a whole. This will allow me to understand if my narrative is sound enough to resonate with those outside of the target audience.

With my story, I hope to reach two main groups. First, I’d like philanthropists who possess a deep appreciation for the artform of theatre to see this and understand that theatre organizations around the country require support in order for Broadway itself to thrive. Although, I believe the overall tone of the project would benefit from being geared specifically toward the second group: producers who may not be privy to the fact that potential Broadway shows don’t always start in New York; for many, the production process begins across the nation. Overall, Broadway investors and philanthropic Broadway lovers must recognize that funding these non-profit organizations directly funds the future of Broadway. If they want Broadway to thrive (and for producers specifically, if they want to continue to have substantial ROIs), they must recognize that long-term sustainability for the industry means investing in these non-profit organizations first.

## Interview script

Goal: 
Have a cohesive narrative throughout the project 

Question: 
Is there cohesion within the overall narrative, or are there moments of the story that lack relevance or don’t have enough contextual information to explain why it is necessary in this context? 

Goal: 
Have sufficient data to fully explain the narrative 

Question: 
Is the data from the Forms 990 (ticket sales vs total expenses) enough to explain the narrative/ point I am trying to get across, or is there additional information from the Forms 990 that I should include to make my argument stronger? 

Goal: 
Integrate additional contextual information/data in order to paint a fuller picture of the overarching story and provide a clear pipeline to the call-to-action 

Question: 
Do you believe that there needs to be any additional contextual information/data provided in order to paint a fuller picture of the overarching story, i.e. callout statistics on specific components of the production process? Additionally, as it stands with the data currently provided, is there a clear pipeline to the call-to-action? 

## Interview findings

Question 1: Is there cohesion within the overall narrative, or are there moments of the story that lack relevance or don’t have enough contextual information to explain why it is necessary in this context? 

Interview 1 (Writer & Producer, 60s)
Yes, the narrative is very cohesive and I like what you have. The only thing I would consider adding - although it might widen your scope too much - is the part of the journey AFTER the show is enhanced or picked up by commercial producers for a Broadway run. Nonprofits are finding ways to participate in commercial runs in various interesting ways. 

Interview 2 (University Administrator/Non-Theatre Background, 50s)
I think it reads well! I suggest highlighting the benefits of investing early- consider tangible evidence of the successes of past productions.

Interview 3 (Arts Administrator, 20s)
I was able to easily follow the story you were telling. I feel like you could include some specific examples of work done by one or more of the theaters you're mentioning in your project though. I feel like that'll help "raise the stakes" within the story.  

Question 2: Is the data from the Forms 990 (ticket sales vs total expenses) enough to explain the narrative/ point I am trying to get across, or is there additional information from the Forms 990 that I should include to make my argument stronger? 

Interview 1 (Writer & Producer, 60s)
Consider comparing revenue to total expenses as well- that could help amplify the need to invest in them even more. 

Interview 2 (University Administrator/Non-Theatre Background, 50s)
It may be worth it to include contributed income of these orgs- although that may derail from the overall story you're crafting. 

Interview 3 (Arts Administrator, 20s)
I think what you have is all you need to get your point across. I do think you could have some small data points here and there though to make a fuller story.

Question 3: Do you believe that there needs to be any additional contextual information/data provided in order to paint a fuller picture of the overarching story, i.e. callout statistics on specific components of the production process? Additionally, as it stands with the data currently provided, is there a clear pipeline to the call-to-action? 

Interview 1 (Writer & Producer, 60s)
The CTA is very clear. If your intended audience are producers, think about adding some stats or information about a famous one. They'll recognize them for sure. 

Interview 2 (University Administrator/Non-Theatre Background, 50s)
I don't think there's any confusion as to what you're trying to get your audience to do! I'll repeat that highlighting the benefits of investing early could be beneficial. 

Interview 3 (Arts Administrator, 20s)
Very good call to action. Again, including some work done by one or more of theatres (and it's accolades potentially) could strengthen your project overall. 

# Identified changes for Part III

Research synthesis:                                       
1) There needs to be an incorporation of specific elements from at least one of the organizations I include in the project (past productions/Broadway transfers, etc). 

2) There needs to be an ample amount of callout statistics and contextual information included in order to paint a fuller picture and support my central narrative. 

3) More data from the Forms 990 need to be included in order to provide more context on the organizations I've chosen to include in the project. 

 Anticipated changes for Part III 

1) As suggested in my interviews and Professor Goranson, I will include a specific show and its accolades into the project to tell a stronger story with a "main character" of sorts. This show will be one that was produced at one of the organizations mentioned and transferred to Broadway shortly thereafter.

2) I will include callout statistics about the Broadway industry as a whole, the organizations I've included, and a quote/stats from a Broadway producer.

3) I will make a second graph in Tableau that compares the 2025 Revenue and Total Expenses for the 5 organizations. 

Overall, the interview process really opened my eyes to what I needed to incorporate in this project to make it stronger and more cohesive. It's always good to have other people look at your work with fresh eyes. Though there were many great suggestions, such as including additional information on the journey shows have once they've been picked up by commercial producers, for the scope of this project, I have decided not to include that. Additionally, it has been great to see my progress using platforms (Tableau, Shorthand, etc.) that I otherwise wouldn't have been able to navigate if I didn't take this course. I definitely feel like the skills I've sharpened here will help me in my career post graduation.
