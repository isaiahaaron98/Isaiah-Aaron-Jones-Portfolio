## Step two: the critique

An element of this data visualization that stool out immediately is the position of the key and its use of grey in some of the wording. Having the key positioned right above the graph limits the effort spent in between digesting what the colors mean and using that knowledge to decipher the graph. In other words, viewers don't have to spend additional time scanning their eyes across the screen because the key and the graph are close in proximity. Furthermore, including the coinciding years of the generations in grey provides pertinent supporting information while not distracting from the overall visualization itself. Additionally, having the percentages only above the 2022 bars in the graph helps the viewer focus on the most recent data presented, which coincides with the callout statistic within the graph. The usage of bold lettering in that statistic and the title also aids in providing visual hierarchy. An element that doesn't work well, however, is how color is used. Since the focus of the visualization is the percentage of U.S. adults who identify as LGBT, it is unsurprising that the creator of the graph chose the colors of the rainbow to help represent the data. However, utilizing red and green together is inaccessible for folks with visual impairments, i.e. red-green color blindness. The x-axis also doesn't have a consistent pattern (2014, 2017, 2020, then 2022), which can be confusing at first glance, and the relationship between the x-axis and the subtitle is a bit confusing. "Share of American adults who identify as LGBT, by birth year" could make someone initially think that the years in the x-axis are the years of birth, which is of course not accurate. Since we're looking at entire generations and not birth years anyway, the subtitle is misleading. In terms of overall clutter, the graphic in the right hand corner does not add anything useful to the visualization, and ultimately serves as a distraction. Finally, since there are no labels on the axes, specifically the y-axis, the percent sign is repeated. If the y-axis was labeled, denoting that the numbers represent percentages, the repetition would not be necessary, freeing up space in the graph. 

The primary audience for this tool seems to be policy makers, as this graph clearly shows that members of the younger generation (Gen-Z) are more likely to identify as LGBT compared to older generations. What this implies is that as time goes on, the LGBT population in the U.S. will continue to increase overall as Gen-Z, and following generations, get older, and the data shows that continuous increase throughout the years. I do believe this visualization is effective for reaching this audience because it clearly conveys how Gen-Z outpaces in LGBT identification compared to older generations, and greater policies will be needed in years to come to protect the rights of the LGBT community. By showing the data of all modern generations though, it provides more context of how the increase of the LGBT population in the U.S., though led by Gen-Z, is contributed by people of all ages.This is important for policy makers to know moving forward, as protective policies for the LGBT community are needed for all age groups, not just young people. 

In my redesign, I will try to focus on colors first and foremost. Having multiple colors for this data is necessary in helping differentiate the generations, but greater care needs to be had in choosing those colors in order to ensure it is accessible for all people. Additionally, I will work on finding a better option other than a cluster bar graph to visualize the data. Although bar graphs are among the easiest types of visualizations to process, clusters can be hard to decipher, especially when some of the data points (particularly the numbers for Gen-X, Boomers, and Traditionalists) are extremely close in value. I'm excited about the ways in which I can place greater focus on pertinent information so that the information is able to be digested in a smaller amount of time. Desaturating the colors for some of the data points, for instance, could help in making the more recent data more prevalent for the viewer. Additionally, creating a dashboard, where each generation has it's own graph, could be an exciting prospect in transforming the data. Also, I will work on the title/subtitle so that they are less misleading and have more specificity, ultimately creating a stronger central narrative. Labeling the axes and taking away clutter, i.e. the graphic in the upper righthand corner, are also elements that I am excited to work on in order to strengthen this data visualization overall.
